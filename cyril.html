St Cyril of Alexandria, Pillar of Faith

"...one Christ and one Son; for the difference of the natures is not taken away by the union." Second Letter to Nestorius

✦ ✦ ✦

Red MonasteryCoptic Orthodox · Sohag

The Coptic Synaxarium calls St Cyril "the pillar of faith and the lamp of the Orthodox church." He was the twenty-fourth Pope of Alexandria, from 412 to 444, and the theologian whose teaching on Christ the Oriental Orthodox Churches have made their own. When they confess "one incarnate nature of God the Word," they are using his words. When they call the Virgin Theotokos, they are standing where he stood at Ephesus.

This is the third page in the series on the Oriental pillars. St Athanasius defended the Son's divinity against Arius; St Basil defended the Holy Spirit. Cyril defended the unity of Christ: that the eternal Son of God is the one who was born of Mary, suffered on the cross and rose, and that He is one, not two persons joined together. Eastern and Oriental Orthodox both honour him as a saint and teacher. Where they part is over whether the Council of Chalcedon in 451 kept faith with him.

Cyril was also a forceful and controversial man, and his early years in Alexandria were marked by violence he has been blamed for. The page does not hide this. It sets out what the ancient sources say in Section XIV.

"...the divinity and the humanity make perfect for us the one Lord Jesus Christ by their ineffable and inexpressible union." St Cyril, Second Letter to Nestorius

A Note on Quotations

Every quotation marked VERBATIM has been checked word for word against a public domain translation: the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers for the letters read at Ephesus and for the historian Socrates; P. E. Pusey's translations of Against Nestorius, That Christ is One and the Scholia (1881); the Library of the Fathers translation of the Commentary on John (1874–1885); and R. Payne Smith's translation of the Commentary on Luke (1859). Where no public domain translation exists, the teaching is summarised and marked PARAPHRASE. Scripture follows the NKJV.

Theotokos
Θεοτόκος: "she who gave birth to God"
Mary's title, defended by Cyril and upheld at Ephesus in 431. It says that the one she bore is God the Word.
Christotokos
Χριστοτόκος: "she who gave birth to Christ"
The title Nestorius offered instead, because he would not say that God was born of a woman.
Hypostasis
ὑπόστασις
A concrete, real subject. Cyril insists that in Christ there is one hypostasis, the Word, who made the flesh His own.
Physis
φύσις: "nature"
Cyril often uses it, as in "one nature of the Word incarnate," for a concrete reality, close in meaning to hypostasis. This is the key to reading his formula rightly.
Hypostatic union
ἕνωσις καθ᾽ ὑπόστασιν
Cyril's phrase for the union of Godhead and manhood in Christ: a real union in one subject, not a moral connection between two.
Mia physis
μία φύσις τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη
"One incarnate nature of God the Word": Cyril's formula, the watchword of the Oriental Orthodox.
Synapheia
συνάφεια: "connection"
Nestorius' word for how God and man are joined in Christ. Cyril rejected it as too weak, a joining of two rather than the becoming of one.
Twelve Chapters
the Twelve Anathemas
The twelve propositions Cyril sent with his Third Letter to Nestorius in 430, each ending "let him be anathema."
Formula of Reunion
AD 433
The statement of faith agreed between Cyril and John of Antioch, which ended the schism after Ephesus.
Antiochene school
Diodore, Theodore, Nestorius
A tradition of theology centred on Antioch that stressed the full humanity of Christ and the distinction of the natures, sometimes to the point of dividing Him.

Cyril was born around 378 in Egypt. He was the nephew of Theophilus, Pope of Alexandria, who trained him for the service of the Church.

c. AD 378
Born in Egypt, probably at Theodosiou in the Delta.
AD 403
Attends the Synod of the Oak with his uncle Theophilus, which deposed St John Chrysostom. Years later Cyril restored Chrysostom's name to the commemorations.
AD 412
Elected Pope of Alexandria on 17 October, after a contested election, three days after Theophilus' death.
AD 414–415
Violent conflicts in Alexandria between Christians and Jews and between Cyril and the imperial prefect Orestes. In March 415 the philosopher Hypatia is murdered by a Christian mob. See Section XIV.
AD 412–428
Years of writing: the Commentary on John, commentaries on the Old Testament, and Paschal letters each year.
AD 428
Nestorius becomes Archbishop of Constantinople and supports a preacher who denies the title Theotokos.
AD 429
Cyril's Paschal letter and his Letter to the Monks of Egypt defend the Theotokos. He writes to Nestorius.
AD 430
His Second Letter to Nestorius (February). A synod in Rome under Pope Celestine condemns Nestorius (August). Cyril's Third Letter, with the Twelve Chapters (November).
AD 431
The Council of Ephesus. Cyril presides. Nestorius is deposed on 22 June. John of Antioch and his bishops, arriving later, hold a rival meeting and depose Cyril. The emperor arrests both sides; Cyril returns to Alexandria in the autumn.
AD 433
Cyril and John of Antioch are reconciled in the Formula of Reunion. Cyril writes, "Let the heavens rejoice."
AD 433–444
Letters explaining his teaching to friends and critics: to Acacius, Eulogius and Succensus. Writes against Julian the Apostate.
AD 444
Departs on 27 June. The Coptic Church keeps his feast on 3 Abib.

The controversy began with a sermon. The historian Socrates, a layman of Constantinople who lived through these events and had read Nestorius' works, tells how it started.

Socrates Scholasticus
Church History VII.32 (c. AD 440)
"This Anastasius preaching one day in the church said, 'Let no one call Mary Theotocos: for Mary was but a woman; and it is impossible that God should be born of a woman.' These words created a great sensation, and troubled many both of the clergy and laity; they having been heretofore taught to acknowledge Christ as God, and by no means to separate his humanity from his divinity on account of the economy of incarnation..."
Church HistorianVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Nestorius defended his chaplain and preached against the title himself. His concern was real: he wanted to protect God from suffering and change, and to keep Christ's humanity complete. But his solution was to speak of the Word and the man as two, joined by a close "connection," so that the Word did not Himself undergo birth or death. Cyril saw at once what that meant. If the one born of Mary is not God the Word, then God did not become man, did not die for us, and did not rise. The whole Gospel was at stake in a single title.

St Cyril
That Christ is One (c. AD 435)
"...and who innovates what seems good to him and says that the holy Virgin is not Mother of God, but mother of Christ and mother of man, bringing in moreover other things discordant and senseless, upon the right and sincere doctrines of the Catholic Church."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

Cyril's answer runs through all his writings, and it is the faith of Athanasius: the Word did not come into a man; He became man. There are not two subjects in Christ, but one, the eternal Son, who made the flesh His own.

St Cyril
Second Letter to Nestorius (AD 430)
"For we do not say that the nature of the Word was changed and became flesh, or that it was converted into a whole man consisting of soul and body; but rather that the Word having personally united to himself flesh animated by a rational soul, did in an ineffable and inconceivable manner become man, and was called the Son of Man, not merely as willing or being pleased to be so called, neither on account of taking to himself a person, but because the two natures being brought together in a true union, there is of both one Christ and one Son; for the difference of the natures is not taken away by the union, but rather the divinity and the humanity make perfect for us the one Lord Jesus Christ by their ineffable and inexpressible union."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

This letter was read at Ephesus and approved by the council as the faith of Nicaea. Notice three things. The Word was not changed into flesh. His humanity is complete, with "a rational soul." And the difference of the natures "is not taken away by the union." Cyril is as firm against confusion as against division.

John 1:14
"And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."
🔵 Orthodox reading: "The Word became flesh": not "the Word came into a man." The subject who became flesh is the Word Himself.
St Cyril
Against Nestorius II, Prologue (AD 430)
"Yet how is it not obvious to all that the Only-Begotten being God by Nature has been made man, not by connection simply (as he says) considered as external or accidental, but by true union, ineffable and passing understanding. And thus He is conceived of as One and Only, and every thing said befits Him and all will be said of One Person."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)
St Cyril
Third Letter to Nestorius, Anathema 3 (AD 430)
"If anyone shall after the [hypostatic] union divide the hypostases in the one Christ, joining them by that connection alone, which happens according to worthiness, or even authority and power, and not rather by a coming together (συνόδῳ), which is made by natural union (ἕνωσιν φυσικὴν): let him be anathema."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Here Cyril names the difference exactly. Nestorius' "connection" joins two subjects by honour or authority, as a king shares his dignity with a favourite. Cyril's "natural union" means a real union in which there is one subject, the Word made flesh.

Philippians 2:6-8
"who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the likeness of men. And being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross."
🔵 Orthodox reading: The one "in the form of God" is the same one who "humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death." One subject, from glory to the cross.
St Cyril
That Christ is One, closing confession (c. AD 435)
"We believe therefore that One is the Son of God the Father and conceived of in One Person, our LORD JESUS CHRIST, begotten forth of God the Father Divinely as Word before every age and time: in the last times of the age the Same made according to the flesh from forth a woman: and to Him we allot both the God-befitting and the human, and His we say was the birth after the flesh and the suffering upon the cross, for that He made His own the whole that belonged to His Proper flesh, yet hath remained Impassible in the Nature of the Godhead."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

For the fuller Oriental Orthodox case, see Miaphysite Christology and Miaphysitism & Dyophysitism.

The sharpest point between Cyril and Nestorius was the cross. Nestorius would not say that the Word suffered or died; only the man did. Cyril answered that this empties the cross of its power. If a man died, a man's death cannot save the world. If God the Word died in His own flesh, death is undone.

St Cyril
Second Letter to Nestorius (AD 430)
"On this account we say that he suffered and rose again; not as if God the Word suffered in his own nature stripes, or the piercing of the nails, or any other wounds, for the Divine nature is incapable of suffering, inasmuch as it is incorporeal, but since that which had become his own body suffered in this way, he is also said to suffer for us; for he who is in himself incapable of suffering was in a suffering body."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Cyril
That Christ is One (c. AD 435)
"...but since being by Nature God He is conceived of as beyond suffering, and was pleased to suffer in order that He might save those who are subject to decay, He was made like in all things unto them who are on earth and underwent birth after the flesh from forth a woman and made (as I said) His own a body capable of tasting death and living again, in order that Himself abiding Impassible, He might be said to suffer in His own flesh."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

Cyril never says that the divine nature suffered. He says that the Impassible One suffered in His own flesh. The suffering is truly His, because the flesh is truly His. This is a paradox, and Cyril treasured it: the Impassible One suffered.

St Cyril
Third Letter to Nestorius, Anathema 12 (AD 430)
"Whosoever shall not recognize that the Word of God suffered in the flesh, that he was crucified in the flesh, and that likewise in that same flesh he tasted death and that he has become the first-begotten of the dead, for, as he is God, he is the life and it is he that gives life: let him be anathema."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
1 Corinthians 2:8
"which none of the rulers of this age knew; for had they known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Paul says the rulers crucified "the Lord of glory." He does not say they crucified a man joined to the Lord of glory.
🟣 Protestant reading: The verse names the divine Christ by a title while the suffering belongs to His human nature, which most Protestants also confess.
Acts 20:28
"Therefore take heed to yourselves and to all the flock, among which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God which He purchased with His own blood."
🔵 Orthodox reading: "The church of God which He purchased with His own blood." Scripture does not hesitate to call the blood of Christ the blood of God.
1 Peter 4:1
"Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same mind, for he who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin,"
🔵 Shared reading: "Christ suffered for us in the flesh." Cyril's phrase "suffered in the flesh" is Peter's.

For Cyril, the title Theotokos was the test of the whole doctrine. To call Mary the Mother of God is to confess that her Son is God.

St Cyril
Third Letter to Nestorius, Anathema 1 (AD 430)
"If anyone will not confess that the Emmanuel is very God, and that therefore the Holy Virgin is the Mother of God (Θεοτόκος), inasmuch as in the flesh she bore the Word of God made flesh [as it is written, The Word was made flesh] let him be anathema."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Cyril
Commentary on Luke, fragments on chapter 1 (c. AD 430)
"The natures, however, which combined unto this real union were different, but from the two together is one God the Son, without the diversity of the natures being destroyed by the union. For a union of two natures was made, and therefore we confess One Christ, One Son, One Lord. And it is with reference to this notion of a union without confusion that we proclaim the holy Virgin to be the mother of God, because God the Word was made flesh and became man, and by the act of conception united to Himself the temple that He received from her."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PAYNE SMITH TRANSLATION (1859)
St Cyril
Scholia on the Incarnation 28, How the holy Virgin is conceived of as Mother of God (c. AD 431–435)
"He was made therefore Man and is said to have endured Generation after the flesh of a woman, because of His taking of the holy Virgin the Body that was united to Him of a truth: whence we say that the holy Virgin is Mother of God, as having borne Him in fleshly or human wise, albeit that He hath His Generation before the ages out of the Father."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)
Luke 1:43
"But why is this granted to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Elizabeth, filled with the Holy Spirit, calls Mary "the mother of my Lord." The child in Mary's womb is already her Lord.

Notice "from the two together is one God the Son." Cyril could speak of "a union of two natures" when the phrase meant a real union from which one Christ results. What he refused was any language that left two subjects after the union. This matters for the reunion of 433 and for Chalcedon (Sections XI and XII). See Mary: Theotokos & Perpetual Virginity.

Cyril's most famous phrase is "one incarnate nature of God the Word." He believed it came from St Athanasius; modern scholars agree that the text he had was by Apollinaris of Laodicea and circulated under Athanasius' name (see the Athanasius page). What matters is what Cyril meant by it, and he explained himself carefully, ruling out the mixture of natures that Apollinaris' followers taught.

St Cyril
Second Letter to Succensus (c. AD 433), as translated by Pusey
"For even if the Only-Begotten Son of God Incarnate and Made man be said by us to be One, He has not therefore been mixed up (as some please to think) nor has the Nature of the Word passed into the nature of the flesh nor yet that of the flesh into His Nature, but, while each abides and is conceived of in its natural property, He united unspeakably and unutterably shewed us One Nature of the Son, yet (as I said) Incarnate. For not merely of things which are simple by nature is the One rightly used, but also of those which are brought together as compounded; such as is man, of soul and body..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)
St Cyril
Against Nestorius II, Prologue (AD 430)
"...the Incarnate Nature of the Word Himself is after the Union now conceived of as One, just as will reasonably be conceived in regard to ourselves too, for man is really One, compounded of unlike things, soul I mean and body. But it is necessary now too to notify that we say that the Body united to God the Word is ensouled with a reasonable Soul."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

The analogy is Cyril's own. A human being is one, though made of soul and body, which are different in kind. No one says a man is two persons, and no one says his body has turned into his soul. So Christ is one, from Godhead and manhood, each remaining what it is. "Nature" in Cyril's formula means the one concrete reality of the incarnate Word, not a blend of divinity and humanity. That is why the Oriental Orthodox reject Eutyches just as firmly as Nestorius.

The Live Coal

Isaiah 6:6-7
"Then one of the seraphim flew to me, having in his hand a live coal which he had taken with the tongs from the altar. And he touched my mouth with it, and said: 'Behold, this has touched your lips; Your iniquity is taken away, And your sin purged.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Cyril saw in the live coal a type of the incarnate Word: wood and fire united, the wood remaining wood yet wholly filled with fire.
St Cyril
Scholia on the Incarnation 9 (c. AD 431–435)
"For as fire having to do with wood and entering into it, seizes hold of it, and removes it not from being wood, but transmutes it rather into the appearance and force of fire, and inworks all its own property therein, and it is now reckoned one with it, so shall you conceive of Christ too. For God united ineffably with the manhood, hath kept it what we say that it is, and Himself hath remained what He was; but once united, is accounted one with it, making His own what is its, and Himself too introducing into it the operation of His own Nature."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

The image of the coal runs through Coptic worship, where the Virgin is praised as the golden censer that carried the burning coal. The fire did not consume the censer, and the Godhead did not consume the manhood.

Cyril's Christology is not abstract. It is the faith behind every Divine Liturgy. Because the flesh of Christ is the Word's own flesh, it is life-giving, and those who receive it in the Eucharist receive life.

John 6:53-54
"Then Jesus said to them, 'Most assuredly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you. Whoever eats My flesh and drinks My blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up at the last day."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Cyril reads this of the Eucharist: the flesh we receive is the flesh of the Word, and so gives eternal life.
🟣 Protestant reading: Many Protestants read the discourse as a call to believe in Christ, with eating as an image of faith.
St Cyril
Commentary on John IV, on John 6:54 (c. AD 425)
"And since the Flesh of the Saviour hath become life-giving (as being united to That which is by Nature Life, the Word from God), when we taste It, then have we life in ourselves, we too united to It, as It to the indwelling Word."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · LIBRARY OF THE FATHERS (1874–1885)
St Cyril
Commentary on John IV, on John 6:54 (c. AD 425)
"And verily when He was raising the little daughter of the chief of the Synagogue saying, Maid, arise, He laid hold of her hand, as it is written, giving life, as God, by His All-Powerful command, and again, giving life through the touch of His Holy Flesh, He shews that there was one kindred operation through both. ... And if by the touch alone of His Holy Flesh, He giveth life to that which is decayed, how shall we not profit yet more richly by the life-giving Blessing when we also taste It? For It will surely transform into Its own good, i. e., immortality, those who partake of It."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · LIBRARY OF THE FATHERS (1874–1885)

"One kindred operation through both": the word and the touch, the divine command and the human hand, are one act of the one Christ. This is the root of the Oriental Orthodox confession of one theandric operation in Christ.

One Bread, One Body

For Cyril the Eucharist does not only unite each believer to Christ. It makes the Church one body.

St Cyril
Commentary on John XI, on John 17:20-21 (c. AD 425)
"For by one Body, that is, His own, blessing through the mystery of the Eucharist those who believe on Him, He makes us of the same Body with Himself and with each other. For who could sunder or divide from their natural union with one another those who are knit together through His holy Body, Which is one in union with Christ? For if we all partake of the one Bread, we are all made one Body; for Christ cannot suffer severance. Therefore also the Church is become Christ's Body..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · LIBRARY OF THE FATHERS (1874–1885)
St Cyril
Commentary on John XI, on John 17:23 (c. AD 425)
"For the Son dwells in us in a corporeal sense as Man, commingled and united with us by the mystery of the Eucharist; and also in a spiritual sense as God, by the effectual working and grace of His own Spirit, building up our spirit into newness of life, and making us partakers of His Divine Nature."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · LIBRARY OF THE FATHERS (1874–1885)
St Cyril
Third Letter to Nestorius, Anathema 11 (AD 430)
"Whosoever shall not confess that the flesh of the Lord gives life and that it pertains to the Word of God the Father as his very own, but shall pretend that it belongs to another person who is united to him [i.e., the Word] only according to honour, and who has served as a dwelling for the divinity; and shall not rather confess, as we say, that that flesh gives life because it is that of the Word who gives life to all: let him be anathema."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
John 6:63
"It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Cyril comments that flesh considered alone "profits nothing," but the flesh united to the life-giving Word becomes life-giving.

The emperor Theodosius II summoned a council to Ephesus for Pentecost 431. Cyril arrived with some fifty Egyptian bishops. Nestorius was there with his supporters. John of Antioch and the Syrian bishops were delayed. After waiting about two weeks past the appointed date, Cyril opened the council on 22 June without them, over the protests of some bishops and the imperial commissioner. This decision has been criticised ever since, and it is fair to say so.

Nestorius refused three summonses to attend. The council read the Creed of Nicaea and Cyril's Second Letter, judged the letter to agree with Nicaea, read extracts from Nestorius' sermons, and deposed him the same day.

The Council of Ephesus
Decree against Nestorius (22 June 431)
"As, in addition to other things, the impious Nestorius has not obeyed our citation, and did not receive the holy bishops who were sent by us to him, we were compelled to examine his ungodly doctrines. We discovered that he had held and published impious doctrines in his letters and treatises, as well as in discourses which he delivered in this city, and which have been testified to."
Ecumenical CouncilVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

When John arrived a few days later, he and his bishops held their own meeting and deposed Cyril. The legates of Pope Celestine, arriving in July, joined Cyril's council. The emperor, unsure what to do, briefly arrested Cyril, Memnon of Ephesus and Nestorius together. In the end Nestorius was sent back to his monastery in Antioch, and Cyril returned to Alexandria.

The council's decision was received by the Church. Ephesus is counted as the third ecumenical council by Oriental Orthodox, Eastern Orthodox and Catholics alike. Its seventh canon forbade anyone to compose "a different Faith as a rival" to the Creed of Nicaea. See Understanding the Nicene Creed.

After Ephesus the Church was split between Alexandria and Antioch. Two years of patient negotiation followed. John of Antioch accepted the deposition of Nestorius, and sent Cyril a confession of faith which called Mary Theotokos and confessed one Christ, while speaking of "a union of two natures." Cyril accepted it.

Psalm 96:11
"Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad; Let the sea roar, and all its fullness;"
🔵 Shared reading: Cyril opened his letter of reconciliation to John with this verse.
St Cyril
Letter 39, to John of Antioch (AD 433), as translated by Pusey
"Let the heavens rejoice and the earth be glad. For the middle-wall of partition is dissolved; what saddened has ceased; all manner of discord is removed. For Christ, the Saviour of us all, has bestowed peace upon all His Churches."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · PUSEY'S TRANSLATION (1881)

Did Cyril give ground? Some of his own supporters thought so, and the monk St Isidore of Pelusium, whom he deeply respected, rebuked him for appearing to contradict himself. Cyril spent the next years explaining that he had not.

St Cyril
Letters to Acacius of Melitene, Eulogius and Succensus (c. AD 433–435)
Cyril explains that the Antiochenes, in speaking of two natures, had not divided Christ: they confessed one Son, one Christ, the Word made flesh, and called the Virgin Theotokos. The two natures may be distinguished in thought, as we distinguish soul and body in a man, but after the union they are not divided, and there is one incarnate nature of the Word. What he had condemned in Nestorius was the division into two sons. That division the Antiochenes had now rejected, and so there was no reason to remain apart.
Coptic Pope & Church FatherPARAPHRASE

This is the same principle Athanasius used with the Antiochenes in 362: ask what the words mean, and if the faith is one, do not divide over the words. Cyril did not withdraw his Twelve Chapters, and the Antiochenes did not have to sign them. They agreed on the one Christ, and the communion was restored.

Cyril died in 444. Seven years later the Council of Chalcedon met, and both the churches that accepted it and those that rejected it claimed Cyril as their teacher. Chalcedon received his Second Letter to Nestorius and his letter to John of Antioch. It also received the Tome of Pope Leo of Rome and defined that Christ is acknowledged "in two natures."

The Oriental Orthodox did not reject Chalcedon because they rejected Cyril. They rejected it because they believed it departed from him. Cyril had accepted talk of "two natures" only in the sense of a union from two, with the natures distinguished in thought. The phrase "in two natures," together with the Tome of Leo's language of each nature doing what is proper to it, sounded to them like the two subjects Cyril had fought. The Council also deposed St Dioscorus, Cyril's successor, who had defended Cyril's formula. The Chalcedonian churches, for their part, held that Chalcedon was faithful to Cyril, and later councils tried to show it.

In the dialogues of 1989 and 1990, Eastern and Oriental Orthodox theologians agreed that both families confess the Christology of Cyril, and that they hold the same faith in different words. Whether the churches will act on that agreement is still being worked out. See The Chalcedonian Schism, Eastern & Oriental Orthodoxy and The Will of Christ.

The Pillar of Faith

In the Coptic Church Cyril is "the pillar of faith." His successors, St Dioscorus and later St Severus of Antioch, saw their whole task as defending his teaching, and Severus' great works are long expositions of Cyril. Every Oriental Orthodox confession of Christ, in Coptic, Syriac, Armenian or Ge'ez, rests on Cyril's formula.

The Liturgy of St Cyril

The Coptic Church preserves a third anaphora, the Liturgy of St Cyril, alongside those of St Basil and St Gregory. It is the Coptic form of the ancient anaphora of Alexandria associated with St Mark, which Cyril is held to have handed on, and it is prayed today on certain occasions in the year.

Teacher of Scripture

Before he was a controversialist, Cyril was an interpreter of the Bible. His commentaries on John and Luke and on many books of the Old Testament fill volumes, and they read every page of Scripture in the light of Christ.

"After dignifying the holy Apostles with the glorious distinction of the apostleship, and appointing them ministers and priests of the Divine Altar, as I have just said, He at once sanctifies them by vouchsafing His Spirit unto them, through the outward sign of His Breath, that we might be firmly convinced that the Holy Spirit is not alien to the Son, but Consubstantial with Him, and through Him proceeding from the Father..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · LIBRARY OF THE FATHERS (1874–1885)

Two things stand out. Cyril sees in the Lord's breathing on the apostles their appointment as "priests of the Divine Altar," with authority to forgive sins. And he speaks of the Spirit "through Him proceeding from the Father." Western writers later cited such phrases for the filioque. In Cyril they express the Spirit's being the Son's own Spirit and His sending through the Son, with the Father as the one source, which is how the Oriental Orthodox read him. See The Filioque.

His Feast

The Coptic Church remembers his departure on 3 Abib. The Synaxarium records that he occupied the throne of St Mark for thirty-one years, eight months and ten days.

Cyril is attacked from several directions: by sceptics for his conduct in Alexandria, by those who think Nestorius was treated unfairly, and by those who think "one nature" means a mixture. Each is answered here as fairly as the evidence allows.

OO Response: Hypatia, a respected Neoplatonist philosopher, was murdered in Alexandria in March 415 by a Christian mob led by a church reader named Peter. The closest source is Socrates, who was no partisan of Cyril. He blames the political quarrel between Cyril and the prefect Orestes, and the rumour among Christians that Hypatia was keeping Orestes from being reconciled to the bishop.

Socrates does not say Cyril ordered the killing, and no ancient source says so. But he does say that the crime "brought not the least opprobrium, not only upon Cyril, but also upon the whole Alexandrian church." In the Greek this means it brought great shame, and Socrates adds that "nothing can be farther from the spirit of Christianity than the allowance of massacres, fights, and transactions of that sort." The Orthodox answer is not to deny the shame. The Church does not canonise saints because they were faultless, and Cyril's early years as bishop show a man who wielded power harshly. He is honoured for his defence of the faith, which the Church judged to be true.

The decisive point came two years later. John of Antioch himself accepted Nestorius' deposition and confessed the Theotokos in the Reunion of 433. The Church as a whole received Ephesus. Its verdict did not rest on a procedural advantage but on the agreement that followed.

But the question at Ephesus was not Nestorius' private intentions. It was whether the one born of Mary is God the Word, and whether we may say that God the Word suffered in the flesh. Nestorius never accepted that God the Word Himself was born and suffered in the sense Cyril meant; even in his last work he spoke of two natural "persons" united in one "person of union."
