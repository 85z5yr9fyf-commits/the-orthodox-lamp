St Athanasius the Apostolic

"For He was made man that we might be made God." On the Incarnation 54

✦ ✦ ✦

St Mark's CathedralCoptic Orthodox · Cairo

The Coptic Church calls him Athanasius the Apostolic. He was the twentieth Pope of Alexandria, and for forty-five years he was the chief defender of the faith confessed at Nicaea, that the Son of God is "of one essence with the Father." He was driven from his see five times by four emperors, and spent about seventeen of those years in exile.

This page is the first in a series on the Oriental pillars: Athanasius, Basil and Cyril, the Fathers whose teaching shaped the councils the Oriental Orthodox receive. Athanasius comes first because the others build on him. Basil defended the Holy Spirit along lines Athanasius had drawn. Cyril of Alexandria, his successor two generations later, built his defence of the one Christ on Athanasius' writings.

The page lets Athanasius speak for himself: on why God became man, against Arius, on Nicaea, on the Holy Spirit and on the one Christ, on the canon of Scripture, and in his own record of the slanders and exiles he endured.

"For He was made man that we might be made God." St Athanasius, On the Incarnation 54

A Note on Quotations

Every quotation marked VERBATIM has been checked word for word against the public domain translation in the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, second series, volume 4 (Athanasius) and volume 7 (Gregory the Theologian). Where a work has no public domain English translation, such as Athanasius' Letters to Serapion, the teaching is summarised and marked PARAPHRASE. Scripture follows the NKJV.

Homoousios
ὁμοούσιος: "of one essence"
The word of Nicaea: the Son is of the same essence as the Father. Athanasius was its great defender.
Ousia
οὐσία: "essence, being"
What God is. Father, Son and Holy Spirit share one ousia.
Hypostasis
ὑπόστασις: "subsistence"
In Athanasius' day the word could mean either essence or person, which caused real confusion. Later it came to mean the three Persons.
Logos
λόγος: "Word"
The eternal Son, through whom all things were made (John 1:1-3), who became flesh.
Arianism
after Arius, a presbyter of Alexandria
The teaching that the Son was the first and greatest creature, made from nothing, and that "once He was not."
Homoiousios
ὁμοιούσιος: "of like essence"
A middle position of the 350s: the Son is like the Father in essence. Athanasius treated those who held it as brothers who were close to the truth.
Theosis
θέωσις: "deification"
Our sharing in the divine life by grace (2 Peter 1:4). For Athanasius it is the purpose of the Incarnation.
Festal Letter
ἑορταστικὴ ἐπιστολή
The yearly letter in which the Pope of Alexandria announced the date of Pascha. Athanasius used his thirty-ninth to list the books of Scripture.
Meletians
after Meletius of Lycopolis
A schismatic party in Egypt that allied with the Arians against Athanasius.
Pope
πάπας: "father"
The ancient title of the Archbishop of Alexandria. A letter preserved by Athanasius addresses him as "the Blessed pope Athanasius."

Athanasius was born in Egypt around 296 to 298 and was raised in the Church of Alexandria under Bishop Alexander, who made him a deacon and his secretary. His life cannot be separated from the Arian controversy, which began in Alexandria when he was a young man and outlasted him.

c. 296–298
Born in Egypt, probably in or near Alexandria.
c. 318
Arius, a presbyter of Alexandria, begins teaching that the Son is a creature. Bishop Alexander condemns him.
AD 325
The Council of Nicaea. Athanasius attends as Alexander's deacon. Gregory the Theologian later writes that, though not yet a bishop, "he held the first rank among the members of the Council."
AD 328
Elected Pope of Alexandria after Alexander's death, at about thirty years of age.
AD 335
The Synod of Tyre, packed with his enemies, accuses him of murdering a bishop named Arsenius. Arsenius is found alive. Constantine nonetheless exiles Athanasius to Trier in Gaul. First exile, 335–337.
AD 336
Arius dies suddenly in Constantinople on the eve of his planned readmission to communion.
AD 339–346
Driven out again, he takes refuge in Rome with Pope Julius, who receives him as a lawful bishop. Second exile.
AD 356–362
The general Syrianus surrounds his church during a night vigil with thousands of soldiers. He escapes and hides among the monks of the Egyptian desert for six years, writing many of his major works. Third exile.
c. AD 357
Writes the Life of St Antony, after the great hermit's death.
AD 362
Returns under Julian and holds the Council of Alexandria, whose Tome to the Antiochenes reconciles parties divided by words.
AD 362–363
Exiled again by Julian the Apostate, who was trying to revive paganism. Fourth exile.
AD 365–366
Exiled under Valens. Coptic tradition says he hid in his father's tomb. Fifth exile.
AD 367
His thirty-ninth Festal Letter lists the twenty-seven books of the New Testament.
AD 373
Departs on 2 May, aged about seventy-five. The Coptic Church keeps his feast on 7 Pashons.
AD 1973
Portions of his relics are brought to Cairo on his feast day after Pope Shenouda III's visit to Rome, and are kept at St Mark's Cathedral.

On the Incarnation is Athanasius' most famous work and one of his earliest. It asks why the eternal Word took a body, and answers that only the Creator could re-create. Mankind, made by the Word and sharing in Him, had turned away and was sliding back into corruption and death. God could neither let His work perish nor simply set aside the sentence of death.

St Athanasius
On the Incarnation 6 (c. AD 318–335)
"For it were not worthy of God's goodness that the things He had made should waste away, because of the deceit practised on men by the devil. ... So, as the rational creatures were wasting and such works in course of ruin, what was God in His goodness to do? Suffer corruption to prevail against them and death to hold them fast? And where were the profit of their having been made, to begin with?"
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Athanasius
On the Incarnation 8
"But He takes a body of our kind, and not merely so, but from a spotless and stainless virgin, knowing not a man, a body clean and in very truth pure from intercourse of men. For being Himself mighty, and Artificer of everything, He prepares the body in the Virgin as a temple unto Himself, and makes it His very own as an instrument, in it manifested, and in it dwelling."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
Hebrews 2:14
"Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the same, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil,"
🔵 Shared reading: The Word shares our flesh in order to die in it, and by dying to undo death. This is the heart of On the Incarnation.
St Athanasius
On the Incarnation 54
"For He was made man that we might be made God; and He manifested Himself by a body that we might receive the idea of the unseen Father; and He endured the insolence of men that we might inherit immortality."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
2 Peter 1:4
"by which have been given to us exceedingly great and precious promises, that through these you may be partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust."
🔵 Orthodox reading: "Made God" means this: not that we become God by nature, but that by grace we become "partakers of the divine nature." Athanasius' sentence is the classic summary of theosis.
🟣 Protestant reading: Many Protestants read this verse as moral renewal rather than participation in God's life.

Notice what the argument requires. If the one who became man were a creature, as Arius taught, He could not give us a share in the divine life, because He would not have it to give. Athanasius' whole doctrine of salvation depends on the Son being fully God. That is why he fought Arianism for half a century. See Theosis & Deification.

Arius set out his teaching in a popular poem called the Thalia. Athanasius quotes it so that readers can see what was really being taught.

Arius, quoted by St Athanasius
Discourse I Against the Arians 5 (c. AD 340)
"'God was not always a Father.' but 'once God was alone, and not yet a Father, but afterwards He became a Father.' 'The Son was not always;' for, whereas all things were made out of nothing, and all existing creatures and works were made, so the Word of God Himself was 'made out of nothing,' and 'once He was not,' and 'He was not before His origination,'..."
Condemned TeachingVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

"Once He Was Not"

Athanasius' first answer is that Arius' little word "once" gives the game away. "Once" means a time before the Son. But the Son is the Word through whom time and all things were made.

St Athanasius
Discourse I Against the Arians 11
"Say then what was once when the Son was not, O slanderous and irreligious men? If you say the Father, your blasphemy is but greater; for it is impious to say that He was 'once,' or to signify Him by the word 'once.' ... And your other, 'The Son was not before His generation,' is equivalent to saying, 'There was once when He was not,' for both the one and the other signify that there is a time before the Word."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
John 1:1-3
"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made."
🔵 Orthodox reading: "In the beginning was the Word." There was no "once" before the Word, because all things, time included, were made through Him.
Hebrews 1:3
"who being the brightness of His glory and the express image of His person, and upholding all things by the word of His power, when He had by Himself purged our sins, sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Athanasius' favourite image: the Son is the radiance of the Father's glory. A light is never without its radiance, so the Father was never without the Son.

"The Lord Created Me"

The Arians' chief proof text was Proverbs 8:22, which in the Greek Bible reads, "The Lord created me a beginning of His ways for His works." Wisdom is speaking, and Wisdom is Christ (1 Corinthians 1:24). So, they said, Christ was created.

Proverbs 8:22
"'The Lord possessed me at the beginning of His way, Before His works of old."
🔵 Orthodox reading: The Hebrew is usually rendered "possessed" (NKJV), but the Greek Bible the Fathers used read "created." Athanasius accepted the Greek and answered the argument on its own ground.
🟣 Protestant reading: Many modern Protestants read the passage as poetry about God's wisdom, not a direct statement about the Son.
St Athanasius
Discourse II Against the Arians 44–45
"...and by Solomon Wisdom says of Itself with cautious exactness, not 'I am a creature,' but only 'The Lord created me a beginning of His ways for His works,' yet not 'created me that I might have being,' nor 'because I have a creature's beginning and origin.' For in this passage, not as signifying the Essence of His Godhead, nor His own everlasting and genuine generation from the Father, has the Word spoken by Solomon, but on the other hand His manhood and Economy towards us."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

"Created" refers to the Word's becoming man, His "manhood and Economy," not to His divine being. The same rule, that some things are said of Christ as God and some of Him as man, runs through all of Athanasius' reading of Scripture, and it is the rule Cyril would later use (Section IX).

We Do Not Worship a Creature

John 5:23
"that all should honor the Son just as they honor the Father. He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him."
🔵 Orthodox reading: The Son receives the same honour as the Father. If He were a creature, worshipping Him would be idolatry.
St Athanasius
Letter 60, to Adelphius, 3 (c. AD 370)
"We do not worship a creature. Far be the thought. For such an error belongs to heathens and Arians. But we worship the Lord of Creation, Incarnate, the Word of God."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

The Arians worshipped Christ too. Athanasius pressed the point: either they were worshipping a creature, which is paganism, or Christ is not a creature. For the full scriptural case see The Divinity of Christ and The Holy Trinity.

Why did Nicaea use a word that is not in Scripture? Athanasius was in the room, and he tells us. The bishops first tried to use Scripture's own words. The Arians found ways to agree with every one of them.

St Athanasius
De Decretis (Defence of the Nicene Definition) 19 (c. AD 350s)
"The Council wishing to do away with the irreligious phrases of the Arians, and to use instead the acknowledged words of the Scriptures, that the Son is not from nothing but 'from God,' and is 'Word' and 'Wisdom,' and not creature or work, but a proper offspring from the Father, Eusebius and his fellows, led by their inveterate heterodoxy, understood the phrase 'from God?' as belonging to us, as if in respect to it the Word of God differed nothing from us..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Athanasius
De Decretis 20
"...were again compelled on their part to collect the sense of the Scriptures, and to re-say and re-write what they had said before, more distinctly still, namely, that the Son is 'one in essence' with the Father: by way of signifying, that the Son was from the Father, and not merely like, but the same in likeness..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

"From God" was not enough, because Christians too are "from God" (1 Corinthians 8:6). So the bishops wrote "from the essence of God" and "one in essence." The new word did not add to Scripture; it shut the door on a false reading of it. See Understanding the Nicene Creed.

John 10:30
"I and My Father are one.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: "One" is neuter in Greek: not one person, but one thing. The Father and the Son are distinct, yet one in what they are.
John 14:9
"Jesus said to him, 'Have I been with you so long, and yet you have not known Me, Philip? He who has seen Me has seen the Father; so how can you say, 'Show us the Father'?"
🔵 Orthodox reading: To see the Son is to see the Father, because the Son is the Father's own image and radiance, not a likeness made from outside.

Athanasius is remembered as unbending, and on the substance of the faith he was. But he was also careful not to divide Christians who meant the same thing with different words. This side of him matters greatly for the Oriental Orthodox today.

Brothers Who Mean What We Mean

In the 350s a large party of bishops would not say "one in essence" but would say "like in essence." Athanasius did not lump them with the Arians.

St Athanasius
De Synodis (On the Councils) 41 (c. AD 359)
"...those, however, who accept everything else that was defined at Nicæa, and doubt only about the Coessential, must not be treated as enemies; nor do we here attack them as Ario-maniacs, nor as opponents of the Fathers, but we discuss the matter with them as brothers with brothers, who mean what we mean, and dispute only about the word."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

One Hypostasis or Three?

In 362 the Church at Antioch was split. One party spoke of three hypostases in God, the other of one, and each suspected the other of heresy. Athanasius' council at Alexandria questioned both.

The Council of Alexandria under St Athanasius
Tome to the Antiochenes 5–6 (AD 362)
"...they replied, Because they believed in a Holy Trinity, not a trinity in name only, but existing and subsisting in truth, ... and that the Son is coessential with the Father, as the fathers said; while the Holy Spirit is not a creature, nor external, but proper to and inseparable from the Essence of the Father and the Son. ... But they in their turn assured us that they neither meant this nor had ever held it, but 'we use the word Subsistence thinking it the same thing to say Subsistence or Essence;'"
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
The Council of Alexandria under St Athanasius
Tome to the Antiochenes 8 (AD 362)
"...counsel the others also who explain and hold aright, not to enquire further into each other's opinions, nor to fight about words to no useful purpose, nor to go on contending with the above phrases, but to agree in the mind of piety."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Gregory the Theologian
Oration 21, On the Great Athanasius, 35 (c. AD 380)
"He conferred in his gentle and sympathetic way with both parties, and after he had carefully weighed the meaning of their expressions, and found that they had the same sense, and were in nowise different in doctrine, by permitting each party to use its own terms, he bound them together in unity of action."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Athanasius' test was simple: ask what the words mean, and if the meaning is orthodox, do not fight over the words. The Oriental Orthodox have always made the same appeal about Christ. When they confess "one incarnate nature of God the Word," they mean one Christ, God and man, without mixture or change, not a nature in which the humanity is swallowed up. The theological dialogues between the Oriental and Eastern Orthodox in 1989 and 1990 rested on exactly this Athanasian principle. See Eastern & Oriental Orthodoxy and Miaphysite Christology.

Nicaea in 325 said only "And in the Holy Ghost." In the 350s a group in Egypt, whom Athanasius called the Tropici, began teaching that the Son was God but the Spirit was a creature, a kind of higher angel. Bishop Serapion of Thmuis wrote to Athanasius for help.

St Athanasius
Letters to Serapion on the Holy Spirit (c. AD 358–359)
Athanasius answers that the Spirit cannot be a creature, because He sanctifies, gives life and makes us partakers of God, which no creature can do. The Trinity is one and indivisible: whatever the Father does, He does through the Son in the Spirit. To call the Spirit a creature is to break the Trinity apart and to bring a creature into the God in whose Name we are baptized.
Coptic Pope & Church FatherPARAPHRASE

In 362 his council at Alexandria put the point plainly: the Holy Spirit "is not a creature, nor external, but proper to and inseparable from the Essence of the Father and the Son" (Tome to the Antiochenes 5, quoted in Section VII). Athanasius died in 373, before the Council of Constantinople. But the line he drew, that whoever sanctifies and gives life must be God, is the line St Basil took up in On the Holy Spirit, and the line the Creed of 381 confessed when it called the Spirit "the Lord and Giver-of-Life," who "with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified."

Athanasius did not live to see the controversies of Ephesus and Chalcedon. But the way he speaks of Christ is the way Cyril of Alexandria and the Oriental Orthodox speak of Him: one subject, the Word Himself, who does divine things and suffers human things in His own flesh.

St Athanasius
Discourse III Against the Arians 30
"And He became man, and did not come into man; for this it is necessary to know, lest perchance these irreligious men fall into this notion also, and beguile any into thinking, that, as in former times the Word was used to come into each of the Saints, so now He sojourned in a man, hallowing him also, and manifesting Himself as in the others."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Athanasius
Discourse III Against the Arians 31
"And on account of this, the properties of the flesh are said to be His, since He was in it, such as to hunger, to thirst, to suffer, to weary, and the like, of which the flesh is capable; while on the other hand the works proper to the Word Himself, such as to raise the dead, to restore sight to the blind, and to cure the woman with an issue of blood, He did through His own body. And the Word bore the infirmities of the flesh, as His own, for His was the flesh; and the flesh ministered to the works of the Godhead, because the Godhead was in it, for the body was God's."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
The Council of Alexandria under St Athanasius
Tome to the Antiochenes 7 (AD 362)
"Wherefore neither was there one Son of God before Abraham, another after Abraham: nor was there one that raised up Lazarus, another that asked concerning him; but the same it was that said as man, 'Where does Lazarus lie;' and as God raised him up: the same that as man and in the body spat, but divinely as Son of God opened the eyes of the man blind from his birth..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

"Not one... another... but the same." This is the single subject that Nestorius would later deny and Cyril would defend. The same Tome insists that "the Saviour had not a body without a soul," against those who would make His humanity incomplete.

St Athanasius
Letter 59, to Epictetus, 2 (c. AD 371)
"What lower region has vomited the statement that the Body born of Mary is coessential with the Godhead of the Word? Or that the Word has been changed into flesh, bones, hair, and the whole body, and altered from its own nature?"
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Athanasius is just as firm against confusing the two. The Word was not changed into flesh, and His body is not of the same essence as His Godhead. One Christ, without division and without change: this is the faith Cyril received from him and the Oriental Orthodox confess. In the same Discourses he calls the Virgin "Mary Bearer of God" (Discourse III 29). See Mary: Theotokos & Perpetual Virginity.

St Athanasius
Letter 60, to Adelphius, 3 (c. AD 370)
"And we neither divide the body, being such, from the Word, and worship it by itself, nor when we wish to worship the Word do we set Him far apart from the Flesh, but knowing, as we said above, that 'the Word was made flesh,' we recognise Him as God also, after having come in the flesh."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Each year the Pope of Alexandria wrote a Festal Letter announcing the date of Pascha. In his thirty-ninth, in 367, Athanasius listed the books of Scripture. It is the earliest surviving list of exactly the twenty-seven books of the New Testament.

St Athanasius
Festal Letter 39, 5–6 (AD 367)
"Again it is not tedious to speak of the [books] of the New Testament. These are, the four Gospels, according to Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. Afterwards, the Acts of the Apostles and Epistles (called Catholic), seven, viz. of James, one; of Peter, two; of John, three; after these, one of Jude. In addition, there are fourteen Epistles of Paul... And besides, the Revelation of John. These are fountains of salvation, that they who thirst may be satisfied with the living words they contain. In these alone is proclaimed the doctrine of godliness. Let no man add to these, neither let him take ought from these."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Athanasius
Festal Letter 39, 7 (AD 367)
"But for greater exactness I add this also, writing of necessity; that there are other books besides these not indeed included in the Canon, but appointed by the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us, and who wish for instruction in the word of godliness. The Wisdom of Solomon, and the Wisdom of Sirach, and Esther, and Judith, and Tobit, and that which is called the Teaching of the Apostles, and the Shepherd."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Two honest notes. First, Athanasius' Old Testament list is not identical to any modern one. He includes Baruch and the Letter of Jeremiah with Jeremiah, sets Esther among the books to be read, and places Wisdom, Sirach, Judith and Tobit in that second group too. The Coptic Church reads these books in her services, and the Oriental Churches' canons are fuller than Athanasius' list. Second, notice who decides. The canonical books are those "handed down, and accredited as Divine," and even the books for reading were "appointed by the Fathers." Athanasius does not compile the canon by his own judgement; he records what the Church had received. See The Biblical Canon.

St Athanasius
Against the Heathen 1 (c. AD 318–335)
"For although the sacred and inspired Scriptures are sufficient to declare the truth, while there are other works of our blessed teachers compiled for this purpose, if he meet with which a man will gain some knowledge of the interpretation of the Scriptures, and be able to learn what he wishes to know..."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Protestant writers often quote the first half of this sentence. The second half matters as much: the Scriptures are sufficient, and the Church's teachers are given to interpret them. Athanasius' whole defence of Nicaea was a contest over how to read Scripture, which he won by appeal to "the sense of the Scriptures" as the Fathers had received it. See Scripture and Tradition.

Athanasius was the first great friend of Egyptian monasticism. During his third exile the monks hid him for six years, and soon after St Antony's death he wrote the Life of Antony, which carried the monastic ideal across the Roman world. Coptic tradition adds that he was the first Pope to wear the monastic habit.

St Athanasius
Life of St Antony 69 (c. AD 357)
"And once also the Arians having lyingly asserted that Antony's opinions were the same as theirs, he was displeased and angry against them. Then being summoned by the bishops and all the brethren, he descended from the mountain, and having entered Alexandria, he denounced the Arians, saying that their heresy was the last of all and a forerunner of Antichrist."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St Antony the Great, in the Life
Life of St Antony 91
"And divide my garments. To Athanasius the bishop give one sheepskin and the garment whereon I am laid, which he himself gave me new, but which with me has grown old."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

The father of monks left his sheepskin to the defender of Nicaea. The desert and the throne of St Mark stood together against Arius, and that bond between monastery and patriarch has marked the Coptic Church ever since. See Monasticism & the Desert Fathers.

The Murdered Bishop Who Was Alive

At the Synod of Tyre in 335 Athanasius' enemies accused him of murdering a Meletian bishop named Arsenius, and carried about a severed hand as evidence. Athanasius records the plot himself.

St Athanasius
Apology Against the Arians 63 (c. AD 357)
"...and at last that calumny is invented by them, that Macarius had broken a cup, and that a certain Bishop named Arsenius had been murdered by me. Arsenius they placed in concealment, in order that he might seem made away with, when he did not make his appearance; and they carried about a hand, pretending that he had been cut to pieces."
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Arsenius was found alive, and the same Apology preserves letters from bishops who confirmed it. Athanasius was exiled anyway. The charges against him were political, and the emperors who exiled him were, for the most part, the patrons of the Arian party.

Why He Fled

St Athanasius
Defence of His Flight 24 (c. AD 357)
"It was now night, and some of the people were keeping a vigil preparatory to a communion on the morrow, when the General Syrianus suddenly came upon us with more than five thousand soldiers, having arms and drawn swords, bows, spears, and clubs... Now I considered that it would be unreasonable in me to desert the people during such a disturbance, and not to endanger myself in their behalf; therefore I sat down upon my throne, and desired the Deacon to read a Psalm, and the people to answer, 'For His mercy endures for ever'"
Coptic Pope & Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

His enemies called him a coward for hiding. In his Defence of His Flight he answers from Scripture: Jacob fled from Esau, Moses withdrew into Midian for fear of Pharaoh, David fled from Saul, and the Lord Himself "hid Himself, and went out of the temple" when the Jews took up stones (John 8:59).

Matthew 10:23
"When they persecute you in this city, flee to another. For assuredly, I say to you, you will not have gone through the cities of Israel before the Son of Man comes."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Athanasius cites this command. Flight was not cowardice but obedience, and it kept the shepherd alive for the flock.

The Twentieth Pope of Alexandria

The Coptic Church counts Athanasius as the twentieth successor of St Mark. The title "pope" was used of the Archbishop of Alexandria long before it became usual in Rome, and Athanasius' own Apology preserves a letter from a repentant priest addressed "To the Blessed pope Athanasius" (Apology Against the Arians 64). The Coptic Synaxarium remembers him on 7 Pashons as the Pope who stood before the whole world for the faith of Nicaea.

The Father of the Ethiopian Church

Athanasius consecrated Frumentius, known in Ethiopia as Abba Salama, as the first bishop of Axum. From that time the Ethiopian Church received its archbishops from Alexandria, until it became autocephalous in the twentieth century. The clearest evidence for the consecration comes from Athanasius' enemy, the Emperor Constantius, who wrote to the kings of Axum demanding that Frumentius be sent to Egypt to be examined by the Arian bishop George.

Ezana is the king remembered in Ethiopia as the first Christian ruler of Axum. The letter shows the Arian emperor trying to pull the young Ethiopian Church away from Athanasius. There is no sign that it succeeded. See History of the Oriental Orthodox Church.

The Teacher of St Cyril

Cyril of Alexandria, the twenty-fourth Pope, is the theologian of the Oriental Orthodox Christology. His writings appeal again and again to Athanasius as a father of the faith, and his single-subject Christology is Athanasius' own: the Word made flesh is the one who acts and suffers (Section IX). The line from Athanasius to Cyril to Dioscorus and Severus is the spine of the Oriental Orthodox tradition.

In Coptic Worship

Athanasius is commemorated in the Coptic liturgy among the great teachers of the Church, and his feast falls on 7 Pashons. In 1973, on that feast day, portions of his relics were brought to Cairo, where they rest at St Mark's Cathedral.
