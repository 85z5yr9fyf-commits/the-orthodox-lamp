Romans 10–11: Israel & the Nations

"For it was not God that cut them off, but they have broken themselves off and fallen." St John Chrysostom, Homily 19 on Romans

Romans 9 is often read as if it stood alone. It does not. Paul's answer to the question "Has God's word to Israel failed?" runs through chapters 10 and 11, and it is only at the end of chapter 11 that he bursts into praise: "Oh, the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God!"

Chapter 10 turns from God's freedom to Israel's responsibility. Israel had zeal but not knowledge, and sought righteousness by the law instead of by faith in Christ, who is the end of the law. The gospel is near: "if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved." But how will people believe unless someone is sent to preach?

Chapter 11 answers whether God has cast away His people. He has not. There is a remnant chosen by grace. Some branches were broken off the olive tree for unbelief, and Gentiles were grafted in by faith, but the broken branches can be grafted in again, and "all Israel will be saved." This page reads these chapters with St John Chrysostom, St Justin, St Augustine and Calvin, and shows how they complete the argument of Romans 9.

"For it was not God that cut them off, but they have broken themselves off and fallen... You see what a great thing a man's free choice is." St John Chrysostom, Homily 19 on Romans

Romans 10:1-4
"Brethren, my heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they may be saved. For I bear them witness that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge. For they being ignorant of God's righteousness, and seeking to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted to the righteousness of God. For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Israel's problem was not lack of zeal but seeking their own righteousness instead of submitting to God's. Christ is the goal of the law for all who believe.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 17 on Romans (on 10:4)
"For if Christ be the end of the Law, he that has not Christ, even if he seem to have that righteousness, has it not. But he that has Christ, even though he have not fulfilled the Law aright, has received the whole. For the end of the physician's art is health."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St John Chrysostom
Homily 17 on Romans (on 10:4)
"For what was the object of the Law? To make man righteous. But it had not the power, for no one fulfilled it. This then was the end of the Law and to this it looked throughout..."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Notice what Paul says the cause of Israel's failure was: "they, being ignorant of God's righteousness, and seeking to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted to the righteousness of God" (10:3). This is the same reason he gave at the end of chapter 9 (9:32). The stumbling was not because God refused them, but because they refused the way He offered. Romans 9 must be read in the light of this.

Romans 10:9-10
"that if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Faith in the heart and confession with the mouth belong together. The confession is that Jesus is Lord and that God raised Him from the dead.
Romans 10:11-13
"For the Scripture says, 'Whoever believes on Him will not be put to shame.' For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord over all is rich to all who call upon Him. For 'whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: No distinction between Jew and Greek: whoever calls on the Lord's name will be saved.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 17 on Romans (on 10:9)
"For that requires the fulfilment of all things (for when you do all, then you shall live); but the righteousness which is of faith does not say this, but what? If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus, and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you shall be saved. Then again that we may not seem to be making it contemptible by showing it to be easy and cheap, observe how he expands his account of it."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St John Chrysostom
Homily 17 on Romans (on 10:6–8)
"...thus, when one is required to believe, there are reasonings which confuse and make havoc of the minds of most men, and it wants a soul of some vigor to shake them thoroughly off."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

In many evangelical churches Romans 10:9 is used as the basis of the "sinner's prayer": a person prays to receive Christ and is assured of salvation. The Orthodox share the conviction behind it, that salvation is near and offered to everyone who believes and calls on the Lord. But in the early Church "Jesus is Lord" was the confession made at baptism, and the Orthodox still have catechumens confess the faith before they are baptised. Confession is not a one-time formula but the beginning of a life, as Christ said: "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father" (Matthew 7:21).

Romans 10:14-15
"How then shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? And how shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: 'How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the gospel of peace, Who bring glad tidings of good things!'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Calling depends on believing, believing on hearing, hearing on a preacher, and preaching on being sent.
Romans 10:17
"So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Faith comes by hearing the word of God.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 18 on Romans (on 10:14–15)
"Yet hear they did, he implies. Then another objection again. And how could they hear without a preacher? Then an answer again. Yet preach they did, and there were many sent forth for this very purpose."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Paul's chain ends with "unless they are sent." The Orthodox see here one of the roots of apostolic order: the gospel is preached by those whom Christ sends through His Church, as the apostles sent others and laid hands on them. Protestants rightly answer that every Christian is called to bear witness. Both are true; the question of who is sent to preach and lead the Church is treated on Apostolic Succession.

"Faith comes by hearing" also explains why the Orthodox Liturgy is so full of Scripture read and sung aloud. For most of Christian history most believers could not read. They heard the word in church, and it formed their faith.

Romans 10:20-21
"But Isaiah is very bold and says: 'I was found by those who did not seek Me; I was made manifest to those who did not ask for Me.' But to Israel he says: 'All day long I have stretched out My hands To a disobedient and contrary people.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: God was found by Gentiles who did not seek Him, and all day long He stretched out His hands to a disobedient Israel.

That last verse is a picture of the potter of Jeremiah 18: God's hands are not closed against Israel but stretched out all day long. The refusal is theirs.

Romans 11:1-2
"I say then, has God cast away His people? Certainly not! For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin. God has not cast away His people whom He foreknew. Or do you not know what the Scripture says of Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel, saying,"
🔵 Orthodox reading: God has not cast away His people. Paul himself is the proof.
Romans 11:5-6
"Even so then, at this present time there is a remnant according to the election of grace. And if by grace, then it is no longer of works; otherwise grace is no longer grace. But if it is of works, it is no longer grace; otherwise work is no longer work."
🔵 Orthodox reading: As in Elijah's day, there is a faithful remnant, chosen by grace and not by works.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 18 on Romans (on 11:5)
"Observe that each word maintains its own rank, showing at once God's grace, and the obedient temper of them that receive salvation. For by saying election, he showed the approval of them, but by saying grace, he showed the gift of God."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St John Chrysostom
Homily 18 on Romans (on 11:4–5)
"...since in Elijah's time the part to be saved had come down to seven thousand: and now also there are probably many that believe. But if you do not know who they are, this is no wonder, for that prophet, who was so great and good a man, did not know."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
Romans 11:7-8
"What then? Israel has not obtained what it seeks; but the elect have obtained it, and the rest were blinded. Just as it is written: 'God has given them a spirit of stupor, Eyes that they should not see And ears that they should not hear, To this very day.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: The rest were hardened, and God gave them "a spirit of stupor."
St John Chrysostom
Homily 19 on Romans (on 11:8)
"Now whence came this blinding? He had indeed mentioned the causes of it before, and turned it all upon their own heads, to show that it was from their unseasonable obstinacy that they had to bear this... And the He gave, do not imagine to mean here an agency, but a permission only."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

This is the same rule St John of Damascus gave for Romans 1 and Romans 9: Scripture often speaks of what God permits as if He did it. The hardening of Israel in 11:7–10 is God's judgement on a refusal, not the cause of it. And, as the next verses show, it is not final.

Romans 11:11-12
"I say then, have they stumbled that they should fall? Certainly not! But through their fall, to provoke them to jealousy, salvation has come to the Gentiles. Now if their fall is riches for the world, and their failure riches for the Gentiles, how much more their fullness!"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Israel's stumbling is not a fall beyond recovery. It opened the door to the Gentiles, and Israel's fullness will mean even greater riches.
Romans 11:17-18
"And if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive tree, were grafted in among them, and with them became a partaker of the root and fatness of the olive tree, do not boast against the branches. But if you do boast, remember that you do not support the root, but the root supports you."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Gentile believers are wild branches grafted into Israel's olive tree. They must not boast against the natural branches.
Romans 11:19-21
"You will say then, 'Branches were broken off that I might be grafted in.' Well said. Because of unbelief they were broken off, and you stand by faith. Do not be haughty, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, He may not spare you either."
🔵 Orthodox reading: The branches were broken off because of unbelief, and you stand by faith. Do not be haughty, but fear.
Romans 11:22-23
"Therefore consider the goodness and severity of God: on those who fell, severity; but toward you, goodness, if you continue in His goodness. Otherwise you also will be cut off. And they also, if they do not continue in unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again."
🔵 Orthodox reading: God's goodness to those who continue in it, His severity to those who fell, and the promise that the broken branches can be grafted in again if they do not continue in unbelief.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 19 on Romans (on 11:23)
"For it was not God that cut them off, but they have broken themselves off and fallen, and he did well to say have broken themselves off. For He has never yet so... cast them off, though they have sinned so much and so often. You see what a great thing a man's free choice is, how great the efficacy of the mind is. For none of these things is immutable, neither your good nor his evil."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION
St John Chrysostom
Homily 19 on Romans (on 11:22–23)
"The reason why He cut you off in severity was, that you might long to come back. The reason why He showed goodness to you was, that you might continue in (he does not say the faith, but) His goodness, that is, if you do things worthy of God's love toward man."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Here, in Paul's own words, is the potter's house of Jeremiah 18. The branches broken off can be grafted back; the branches grafted in can be cut off. God's severity aims at return; His goodness asks for perseverance. "None of these things is immutable." This is the end of the argument that began in Romans 9, and it confirms that the potter of 9:21 is the potter who remakes the spoiled vessel.

Calvin, who read Romans 9 as a decree concerning individuals, reads the condition in 11:22 as applying to peoples, not to the elect individually:

John Calvin
Commentary on Romans (on 11:22)
"But as he speaks not of the elect individually, but of the whole body, a condition is added, If they continued in his kindness... but it would be improper to say of any one of the godly particularly, that God had mercy on him, when he chose him, provided he would continue in his mercy; for the perseverance of faith, which completes in us the effect of God's grace, flows from election itself."
ReformerVERBATIM · OWEN TRANSLATION (1849)

This is worth noticing. When the text is conditional, Calvin says it speaks of peoples, not individuals; when the text speaks of peoples in Romans 9 (Jacob and Esau, "two nations"), he reads it as a decree about individuals. The Orthodox read both chapters the same way: God deals with peoples and with persons, and in both cases He responds to faith and unbelief. Calvin is right, though, about one thing: these warnings are meant to humble, not to terrify. Even Calvin says here that those who "begin to loathe the kingdom of heaven" deserve to be blinded again.

Romans 11:25-27
"For I do not desire, brethren, that you should be ignorant of this mystery, lest you should be wise in your own opinion, that blindness in part has happened to Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And so all Israel will be saved, as it is written: 'The Deliverer will come out of Zion, And He will turn away ungodliness from Jacob; For this is My covenant with them, When I take away their sins.'"
🔵 Orthodox reading: Israel's hardening is partial and temporary, until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in, and so all Israel will be saved.

Who is "all Israel"? There have been three main answers.

If then this has been promised, but has never yet happened in their case, nor have they ever enjoyed the remission of sins by baptism, certainly it will come to pass."

3. Israel on a separate track

In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries many evangelical Protestants adopted dispensationalism, which teaches that God has two distinct peoples with distinct destinies, Israel and the Church, and that Romans 11 refers to a future national restoration connected with the rapture and a millennium. This view is unknown in the Fathers and is discussed on The Rapture & End Times. A few modern writers go further and suggest that Jews may be saved by the old covenant apart from faith in Christ. Paul's own words rule this out: they will be grafted in again "if they do not continue in unbelief" (11:23).

The Orthodox hold together what the Fathers held: the Church, made of Jews and Gentiles in Christ, is the Israel of God; and the Jewish people remain "beloved for the sake of the fathers" (11:28), and the Church prays and hopes for their turning to Christ, which many Fathers expected before the end.

A Hard Fact

Romans 11 forbids Gentile Christians to "boast against the branches." It must be said honestly that Christians have often done exactly that. St John Chrysostom himself, whose reading of Romans 11 is so generous, preached eight sermons Against the Jews in Antioch in 386 and 387. They were aimed at Christians who were attending synagogue festivals, but their language about the Jewish people is harsh, and in later centuries it was used to justify hatred. The Orthodox honour Chrysostom as a saint and teacher; that does not mean every sentence he spoke in the heat of controversy is the mind of the Church. Paul's command stands over all of us: "Do not be haughty, but fear."

Romans 11:28-29
"Concerning the gospel they are enemies for your sake, but concerning the election they are beloved for the sake of the fathers. For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Israel remains beloved because of the patriarchs. God's gifts and calling are irrevocable.
Romans 11:30-32
"For as you were once disobedient to God, yet have now obtained mercy through their disobedience, even so these also have now been disobedient, that through the mercy shown you they also may obtain mercy. For God has committed them all to disobedience, that He might have mercy on all."
🔵 Orthodox reading: God has shut up all in disobedience so that He may have mercy on all.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 19 on Romans (on 11:32)
"However, they will not perish forever. For God has concluded them all in unbelief, that is, has convinced them, has shown them disobedient; not that they may remain in disobedience, but that He may save the one by the captiousness of the other, these by those and those by these."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

Here the long argument of Romans 9 to 11 reaches its goal. It does not end with some vessels fixed for honour and others for wrath, but with God's purpose to have mercy on all, Jew and Gentile, each brought in by way of the other. That is the context in which Romans 9 must be read.

Does "mercy on all" mean everyone will finally be saved? Origen speculated that in the end all rational beings, perhaps even demons, would be restored. That speculation was rejected, and Pope Theophilus of Alexandria condemned Origen's errors. Paul is speaking of Jews and Gentiles as peoples, and the same chapter says that branches which do not continue in God's goodness will be cut off (11:22). God's will is that all be saved; whether each person accepts that mercy is the freedom He has given. The Church hopes and prays for all, and does not presume to declare that no one will be lost.

Romans 11:33-36
"Oh, the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are His judgments and His ways past finding out! 'For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has become His counselor?' 'Or who has first given to Him And it shall be repaid to him?' For of Him and through Him and to Him are all things, to whom be glory forever. Amen."
🔵 Orthodox reading: Paul's argument ends not in a system but in worship. God's judgements are unsearchable, and all things are from Him, through Him and to Him.
St John Chrysostom
Homily 19 on Romans (on 11:33)
"That it is a depth then, he knows: but how great, he knows not. For the language is that of a person wondering, not of one that knew the whole. But admiring and being awe-struck at the goodliness, so far forth as in him lay, he heralds it forth by two intensitive words, riches and depth, and then is awestruck at His having had both the will and the power to do all this, and by opposites effecting opposites."
Church FatherVERBATIM · NPNF TRANSLATION

This is a good place for every reader of Romans 9 to 11 to end, whatever his tradition. Paul does not finish with a diagram of the decrees. He finishes on his knees. The Orthodox reading of these chapters does not claim to explain everything about God's ways. It claims only what Paul himself says: that God's wisdom brings mercy out of disobedience, that no one is beyond the reach of His outstretched hands, and that "of Him and through Him and to Him are all things, to whom be glory forever. Amen."

OO Response: Paul's point is that the remnant was not chosen because of works of the law; it was God's gift. Chrysostom notes that Paul's two words keep "their own rank": "election" shows the approval of those who received salvation, and "grace" shows the gift of God.

And in the same chapter Paul explains why others were not in the remnant: "because of unbelief they were broken off" (11:20).

OO Response: Paul is quoting Isaiah 29:10 about people who had already refused God's word. Chrysostom: "do not imagine to mean here an agency, but a permission only," and the causes "he had indeed mentioned before, and turned it all upon their own heads."

And the hardening is "in part" and "until" (11:25). God's purpose in it is mercy, not destruction.

OO Response: The Church is the Israel of God, but Paul is clear that God has not cast away His people (11:1), that they are still "beloved for the sake of the fathers" (11:28), and that His gifts and calling are irrevocable (11:29). The Fathers expected Israel's turning to Christ, not its disappearance.

What Paul rules out is a salvation apart from Christ. The natural branches are grafted back into the same tree, the one people of God in Christ.

OO Response: Paul is not describing a formula separate from baptism; he is describing the faith that is confessed at baptism and lived afterwards. In Romans 6 he has already said that "as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death."

Christ Himself warned that saying "Lord, Lord" is not enough without doing the Father's will (Matthew 7:21). Confession with the mouth is real and necessary, and it is the beginning of the Christian life, not the whole of it.

The Church has no doctrine that ties Romans 11 to any modern state. The promises of Romans 11 concern Israel's turning to Christ, not a political event. Orthodox Christians, including many Palestinian and Arab Orthodox in the Holy Land, hold a range of views on the politics of the region, and the Church calls all to justice and peace.

Paul did, at the cost of his life, and said his heart's desire was for their salvation (10:1). The gospel is "for the Jew first" (1:16). Christians are called to share it with love, humility and respect, never with contempt, remembering that they themselves are grafted into Israel's tree.

Paul warns Gentile believers that they too can be cut off "if you do not continue in His goodness" (11:22), and Chrysostom says "none of these things is immutable." Calvin reads the warning as addressed to the Gentiles as a body. The Orthodox take it as a real warning to every believer, given so that we will continue, not so that we will live in fear. See Assurance of Salvation.

"I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God..." Having shown God's mercy, Paul turns to the life that answers it: the living sacrifice, the body of Christ, love, the authorities, the weak and the strong, and his greetings to the saints in Rome. That is Romans 12–16.
